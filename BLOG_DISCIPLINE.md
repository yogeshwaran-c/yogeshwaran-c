# Blog Discipline

Rules that separate bloggers who post 3 times and quit from those who post for 10 years. These are *discipline rules*, not content rules — the habits and rituals that make regular publishing sustainable.

Companion to [BLOG_GUIDE.md](./BLOG_GUIDE.md).

---

## 1. A signature format

Readers follow shapes, not topics. Pick ONE recurring column style and keep using it:

- **"Patch Notes"** — one merged PR per post, always same structure (symptom → wrong guess → real cause → fix → takeaway)
- **"The Debugging Diary"** — monthly roundup of 3-5 bugs with 2-paragraph writeups
- **"1 PR, 1 Lesson"** — short posts, one technical insight each

Pick one. Stick for at least a year. It compounds.

---

## 2. Ship-ugly rule

Publish when it's 80%. Never chase 100%. The post that dies in "one more edit" is the one you never ship. Accept that your October post will look worse than your December post — that's growth, not failure.

---

## 3. The "never miss twice" law

Skipping one month is survivable. Skipping two breaks the habit permanently. If you missed April, you MUST publish in May, even if the post is short and rough. Protect the streak over protecting the quality.

---

## 4. A 3-draft buffer, always

Never publish your only draft. Keep 3 posts in various states:

- 1 polished (ready to publish)
- 1 first draft
- 1 outline

When life gets busy, you still ship. When inspiration hits, you stockpile instead of panic.

---

## 5. Cross-link every post

Every new post references at least 1 previous post. This is how you build a *body of work* instead of a scattered archive. Readers who find one post discover three.

---

## 6. One screenshot-worthy line per post

Every post needs exactly one sentence someone could tweet as a standalone. Not clever — *useful*.

Example: *"If a container has `user-select: text`, every descendant is in your selection by default."*

That's what earns quote-tweets and inbound links.

---

## 7. Living documents, not frozen posts

Technical content goes stale. Add **"Updated YYYY-MM"** notes when things change. Readers trust bloggers who maintain their posts. Freshness also bumps Google rankings.

---

## 8. Ignore stats for 6 months

The first 10 posts get no traffic. That's normal. Checking Plausible/GA daily for a post with 47 views will kill your motivation. Write as if no one reads. At post #12 the flywheel starts.

---

## 9. Own the canonical URL

Your domain is the source of truth. Cross-posts to Hashnode/Dev.to must include `<link rel="canonical">` back to `yogeshwaran.com`. All SEO juice, inbound links, and authority accrue to your domain — not to a platform that may vanish.

---

## 10. A consistent sign-off

Same closing paragraph/line on every post. Builds recognizable brand, signals this is part of an ongoing body of work.

Template:

> *Written after merging [PR #XXXX]. Part of an ongoing series on fixes I've shipped to open source — follow at [yogeshwaran.com](https://yogeshwaran.com).*

Small thing. Massive compound effect on brand recognition.

---

## Quick monthly checklist

Before hitting publish, verify:

- [ ] Uses the signature format
- [ ] Cross-links at least 1 previous post
- [ ] Has one screenshot-worthy takeaway line
- [ ] Canonical URL set correctly
- [ ] Sign-off paragraph present
- [ ] 80% quality bar cleared — don't chase 100%
- [ ] Next draft in the buffer
